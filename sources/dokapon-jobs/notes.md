# Dokapon Kingdom — Jobs / Classes & Character Progression

Research compiled from the Dokapon Fandom Wiki, GameFAQs class FAQs, Steam Community guides (notably the "Minmaxing Dokapon" guide and "The Missing Manual" for Connect), gameplay.tips, and r/Dokapon meta discussion.

## 1. Two-Track Progression: Character Level vs. Job Level

Dokapon Kingdom's progression has two largely independent axes:

- **Character Level** — gained from EXP earned by defeating monsters/players. Hard cap is **Lv. 99** (Sword of Fury / DKC). Reaching Lv. 99 requires roughly **2,630,344 cumulative EXP** — a steep curve that is essentially never hit in a normal-length game.
- **Job Level** — gained by winning **battles while in that job**, capped at **Lv. 6** (= Mastered). Battles-per-level varies by job:
  - **7 battles/level**: Warrior, Magician, Thief, Cleric, Acrobat
  - **8 battles/level**: Spellsword, Alchemist, Ninja, Monk
  - **10 battles/level**: Robo Knight, Hero (slowest to grind)
- Stat gains from character level-ups are **permanent** and carry across job changes; the *amount* gained per level depends on the **current job's growth pattern**, so the order in which you wear jobs matters enormously.

## 2. Stats

Five core stats:

- **HP** — life pool.
- **AT (Attack)** — physical damage with weapons / Strike.
- **DF (Defense)** — physical damage mitigation. Widely considered a **dump stat** because Dokapon's damage formula doesn't favor it; HP is preferred as a sponge.
- **MG (Magic)** — battle magic damage and field magic potency.
- **SP (Speed)** — accuracy of Strike, dodge, and field-magic landing rate. Often called the **dominant stat** because both offense (Strike) and field harassment scale with it.

## 3. Job Change Rules

- Players change jobs at **Dokapon Castle** by talking to the King.
- Mastering prerequisites is the gate for advanced jobs (see class-list.md).
- Some jobs require a **key item** drop, not just mastery (Acrobat → Show Ticket from Casino Cave; Robo Knight → Lost Technology from Underwater Shrine; Hero → Hero License from Castle in the Clouds).

## 4. Mastery Bonuses (the core meta-progression)

When a job hits Lv. 6 it is **Mastered**, granting a **permanent +1 stat per character level-up forever**, even after switching jobs. These stack across mastered jobs. Examples:

| Job | Mastery Bonus |
|---|---|
| Warrior | +1 AT/level |
| Magician | +1 MG/level |
| Thief | +1 SP/level |
| Cleric | +1 DF/level |
| Spellsword | +1 AT/level |
| Alchemist | +1 MG/level |
| Ninja | +1 SP/level |
| Monk | +10 HP/level |
| Acrobat | +10 HP/level |
| Robo Knight | +1 DF/level |
| Hero | +1 AT/level |

This is the chief reason the game rewards **cycling jobs** rather than committing to one early — every mastery permanently steepens your future growth.

## 5. Per-Level Stat Growth (current job)

See `class-list.md` for the full table. High-level pattern:

- Starters give modest, focused growth (Warrior: +2 AT / +1 DF / +10 HP per level).
- Advanced jobs concentrate on two stats heavily (Spellsword +2 AT / +2 MG; Ninja +2 AT / +2 SP).
- Hero is the only "balanced" job (+1 to all four combat stats plus a random bonus stat per level), making it the strongest *per-level* growth in the game.
- Monk and Acrobat trade combat stats for HP gain (+20 and +10 respectively per level), and pair with HP-flavored mastery bonuses.

## 6. Skills: Field & Battle

Every job carries:

- **Field Skill** — passive or use-on-map ability (e.g. Alchemist's Duplicate copies a random item/spell; Thief gets Steal/pickpocket).
- **Two Battle Skills** — learned at job level 2 and level 4. They replace/augment the four base battle commands (Attack, Strike, Defend, Counter, Magic) and are intra-battle only (buffs don't persist).

Notable battle skills frequently cited as build-defining:

- **Monk — Soul Fire**: cited by guides as "one of the best battle skills in the game"; makes Monk practically unbeatable in 2+ turn fights (it ramps damage).
- **Alchemist — Alchemy**: converts damage dealt into **gold**, fueling the Alchemist's gold-snowball gameplan.
- **Alchemist — Debug**: chance to slash opponent HP to 1 — swings PvP fights.
- **Ninja**: SP-based harassment kit, fastest game-clear because of its raw speed/movement and chase potential.

## 7. Item & Magic Inventory by Job

Carry capacity matters because **more items = less random outcomes** on the board (you can plan around bad rolls). Approx. capacities:

- Warrior 6 item / 4 magic, Magician 6/10, Thief 8/6
- Cleric 6/8, Spellsword 8/8, Alchemist 6/10, Monk 8/6, Acrobat 6/6, Ninja 10/6
- Robo Knight 10/8, **Hero 12/12** (largest)

## 8. Stat-Up Books / Shrines

In Dokapon Kingdom there are stat-boosting **books** (Power Book/AT, Spirit Book/MG, Charm Book/SP, Body Book/HP, Defense Book/DF) and **stat shrines / temples** scattered on the world map that grant a one-time permanent stat increase. They're a third stat source beyond level-ups and mastery bonuses, and minmaxers visit shrines aggressively in early weeks. (Specific numerical effects weren't directly captured here — verify on the Fandom Base Stats page.)

## 9. The Darkling — special transformation, not a job-change

- Triggered when a player spends **~2 weeks in last place**, gaining the **Dark Mark**, then lands on the Dark Space in Asiana.
- Cost: **all** of your towns, items, field magic, and gold are surrendered to Weber.
- Lasts **14 days**. Cannot level, has no salary.
- Stats are tripled and you wield unique top-tier gear; gain the **Dark Arts** kit (e.g. **Castle Panic** — steal a castle; the *only* way Darkling grows assets), **auto-Counter** vs Strike, summon Town Bosses, full heal/cure on Town Boss tiles, can fight under Fear status.
- Functions as a **catch-up / villain** mechanic — high variance kingmaker rather than a competitive build.

## 10. Power Balance & "Best Class"

There is no single dominant class; the meta is a soft rock-paper-scissors plus an early-game leader:

- **Thief** is the consensus best **starter** because SP scaling, mobility, Steal field skill, and 8-item bag are all early-game leverage.
- The advanced "triangle":
  - **Ninja** — best speedrun/clear, SP harassment.
  - **Monk** — best sustain, beats Ninja in PvP via Soul Fire.
  - **Alchemist** — gold snowball, beats Monk in long economic games.
  - Ninja loops back to contest Alchemist via SP harassment + game-end pressure.
- **Hero** has the strongest raw growth and salary, but unlocking it requires mastering Spellsword + Ninja + Acrobat *and* a Hero License — by then the run is usually decided.
- **Acrobat** is a niche pick: weak combat growth, but its passive (deception/dodge) is "arguably the best in the game" per community.
- **Robo Knight** is bulky but slow to unlock and DF-focused (a stat the formulas underrate).

Stat priority advice that surfaces repeatedly: **HP and AT** are core; **SP** is the king stat for Strike + field magic; **MG** is build-dependent (mandatory for Magician/Alchemist gameplans); **DF** is skipped in favor of HP.

## 11. Build Diversity / Common Optimal Path

A common minmax route:
1. **Start Thief** (mobility, gold, Steal).
2. **Master Warrior** next for permanent +1 AT/level.
3. **Master Magician** for +1 MG/level.
4. **Master Cleric** (off Warrior/Magician/Thief) for +1 DF/level — quick mastery.
5. Spec into **Spellsword** or **Ninja** for the late game depending on group composition.
6. **Hero** only as a flex if the game drags on.

The "always-on +1 stat per mastered job" mechanic means tempo loss to switch jobs is recouped every subsequent level — so cycling masteries is mathematically dominant over staying single-class once a job is mastered.
